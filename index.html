How this TDEE calculator works and how to use it

This calculator estimates your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) using established basal metabolic rate (BMR) formulas and an activity multiplier. BMR represents the calories your body needs at rest to maintain vital functions. We offer three formulas: Mifflin–St Jeor (recommended for most adults), Harris–Benedict (a revised classic), and Katch–McArdle (which requires body fat percentage and uses lean mass).

To use the tool, enter your age, biological sex, height in centimetres and weight in kilograms. Choose an activity level that best reflects your weekly routine: sedentary, light, moderate, active or athlete. If you know your body fat percentage, select Katch–McArdle for a lean‑mass based estimate. The calculator multiplies BMR by an activity factor to produce TDEE, then applies a macro split to convert calories into grams of carbohydrates, protein and fat.

The default macro split is 45% carbohydrates, 25% protein and 30% fat — a balanced starting point for many people. You can customize macro percentages to match dietary preferences (for example, higher protein for strength training or lower carbs for a low‑carb approach). When adjusting macros, ensure the percentages sum to 100% to keep calorie calculations consistent.

Use the results as a starting guideline. Track your weight and body composition over 2–4 weeks and adjust calories if you are not seeing the expected changes. A safe calorie deficit for weight loss is typically 10–15% of TDEE; for muscle gain, a modest surplus of around 5–10% is common. Remember that individual metabolism varies — factors like sleep, stress, hormones and training intensity influence outcomes. For medical conditions, pregnancy, or elite athletic planning, consult a registered dietitian or medical professional.

This tool is designed for European metric units and provides shareable results so you can save or discuss them with a coach. It is an educational resource and not a substitute for personalized medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total calories your body burns in a day. It combines your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) with all daily activities, exercise, and digestion.

BMR is the energy your body uses at rest. TDEE includes BMR plus activity, so it reflects your real‑life calorie needs more accurately.

You can adjust your calorie intake based on your goal:
– Weight loss: eat about 10–15% fewer calories than your TDEE.
– Maintenance: eat close to your TDEE.
– Muscle gain: eat about 10% more than your TDEE.

Example: A 35‑year‑old male, 180 cm tall, 80 kg, with moderate activity has a TDEE of ~2,500 kcal/day. To lose weight, aim for ~2,200 kcal/day. To gain muscle, ~2,700 kcal/day.
